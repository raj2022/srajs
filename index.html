Experience.

Analysis, detector operations and hardware on CMS.

Lead analyzer

CMS Collaboration, 05/2024 to present

I lead a Run 3 search for resonant scalar pair production, X → YH with Y → bb and H → γγ, in the NMSSM. It is the successor to the Run 2 analysis.

I built the analysis chain: signal categorization, parametric signal and background modelling, and limit extraction with the CLs method. It runs across a mass grid of about 200 points per data-taking era, with automated multi-year combination and batch jobs on HTCondor.

I am guiding the analysis through CMS review ahead of pre-approval, with a paper to follow.

B2G contact and formal reviewer

CMS Muon Physics Object Group, 2025 to present

I am the B2G analysis group's liaison to the Muon POG and carry out formal object-level reviews of muon reconstruction, identification and scale-factor treatment for analyses submitted to the collaboration.

I have completed muon-sector reviews for more than 20 CMS analyses, auditing object definitions, corrections and systematics against the review questionnaire and writing the formal reviewer comments.

Pixel Detector-on-Call

CMS Run 3 operations, 06/2025 to 12/2025

I took part directly in CMS data-taking as the on-call expert for the Pixel sub-detector, monitoring detector and data-quality status in real time and responding to operational issues during collisions.

Graduate researcher, pixel detector development

The Catholic University of America, 05/2023 to present

I assemble 1×2 and 2×2 forward pixel (TFPX) modules with CROC v2 readout chips for the CMS Phase-2 Inner Tracker upgrade. The university is one of four US CMS production sites, with Purdue, Nebraska and Boston University.

The work covers precision gluing of the high-density interconnect on a gantry system, wire-bonding and pull testing (typical strength about 9 cN), and electrical qualification in a cold box at about −10 °C: IV curves, PixelAlive and Shunt-LDO tests.

Projects.

Machine learning and detector work around CMS. Select a project to read more.

Quantization of MLPFMaking the machine-learned particle-flow reconstruction smaller and faster.

MLPF is a graph neural network that reconstructs every particle in a CMS collision. I developed the post-training quantization and ONNX deployment pipeline that reduces its size and inference latency, from 07/2023 to 07/2025.

The work is part of the CMS paper on full event interpretation with ML-based particle-flow reconstruction, published in Eur. Phys. J. C.

Quantization, ONNX, Graph neural networks, PyTorch

Anomaly detection for EGamma data qualityAn autoencoder that flags bad data, offline and in real time.

In 08/2024 to 12/2024 I developed an autoencoder-based pipeline for offline certification of EGamma data, with an online deployment during Run 3 that flags anomalous events as they are recorded.

I also served as the CMS institutional reviewer for the resulting methodology paper, on Wasserstein-normalized autoencoders for anomaly detection.

Autoencoders, Anomaly detection, Data quality monitoring, Real-time deployment

Tracker material validationChecking the detector material in simulation across tracker generations.

From 05/2023 to 08/2024 I validated detector material budgets in simulation across the CMS Phase 0 to Phase 2 tracker geometries and contributed to the CMSSW material-validation software.

CMSSW, Simulation, Validation, Tracker

Master's thesis: a neural-network search for T′Deep learning for heavy vector-like quark resonances.

At NISER (2020 to 2022) I built a deep neural network to search for heavy vector-like quark T′ resonances against Standard Model Higgs and non-resonant backgrounds. I benchmarked it against a mature BDT-based CMS analysis using the Higgs Combined Tool.

Earlier projects included Kalman-filter track fitting for an RPC cosmic-ray muon detector and neutrino oscillation phenomenology.

Deep neural networks, Boosted decision trees, Combine, Kalman filter
