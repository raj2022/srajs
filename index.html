Quantization and sparsificationCompressing neural networks so they run fast enough for real-time use.

Large neural networks are expensive to run. Quantization stores weights and activations with fewer bits, and sparsification removes the weights that contribute least. Together they shrink a model and cut its latency and memory use, which matters when a decision has to be made in a tiny fraction of a second, as in a detector trigger.

Quantization, Pruning, Neural networks, Low latency

Hypergraph models for MLPFMachine-learned particle flow: reconstructing every particle in a collision with a neural network.

Particle flow combines signals from every part of the detector to reconstruct each particle in a collision. MLPF replaces the hand-written rules with a neural network that learns this directly from simulation. Hypergraphs can link more than two elements at once, so they offer a natural way to group all the signals that belong to one particle.

Particle flow, Graph neural networks, Hypergraphs, Reconstruction

Anomaly detection for data qualityFlagging problems in CMS data automatically, before they reach a physics analysis.

CMS checks the quality of its data as it is recorded, and people on shift look through hundreds of monitoring plots. Anomaly detection learns what normal data looks like and flags what doesn't fit, so problems are caught sooner and with less manual effort.

Anomaly detection, Data quality monitoring, Unsupervised learning

fPixThe forward pixel detector of the CMS tracker.

The pixel detector sits closest to the collision point and records the first measurements of every charged particle. Its forward part covers the regions near the beam pipe, where particles leave at shallow angles. Precise tracking there feeds into every physics result that follows.

Pixel detector, Tracking, CMS tracker

HH → γγbbA search for pairs of Higgs bosons decaying to two photons and two b quarks.

Producing two Higgs bosons at once is extremely rare, and its rate depends on how the Higgs boson interacts with itself, which is one of the open questions of the Standard Model. The final state with two photons and two b quarks is rare but clean: the photons give a sharp mass peak to search for above a smooth background.

Higgs physics, Statistical inference, Background modelling, Event selection
